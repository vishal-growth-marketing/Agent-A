# Agent-A Glossary

This glossary defines key terminology used throughout Agent-A.

Use these definitions to maintain consistency in documentation, Skills, Orchestrators, and Governance.

---

## Agent-A

An open-source framework for Human-Governed AI marketing execution. Agent-A organizes AI into specialist marketing roles coordinated through shared governance and human oversight.

---

## Constitution

The permanent operating principles of the Agent-A system. Constitution defines rules that must never change, including command hierarchy, human governance, and permanent operating behavior. Constitution takes precedence over all other documents.

---

## Execution

The shared operational context for every Skill and Orchestrator. Execution documents the current business state, including positioning, ICP, pricing, messaging, and active priorities. Execution changes frequently as business needs evolve.

---

## Execution Playbook

See Execution.

---

## Governance

Shared operating standards that guide every Skill and Orchestrator. Governance includes Constitution, Execution, Quality Gates, Handoff Rules, and Output Standards. Governance ensures consistency across the entire workflow.

---

## Handoff

The structured transfer of work between specialists. A handoff includes previous outputs, relevant assumptions, supporting evidence, confidence level, and recommendations for the next specialist. Every handoff preserves context to prevent rework.

---

## Handoff Rules

Documentation that defines how work should move between specialists, including required information, quality expectations, and validation requirements.

---

## Human-Governed AI

An operating model where humans remain responsible for defining objectives, approving plans, reviewing recommendations, and making final decisions. AI accelerates execution within defined responsibilities.

---

## ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

A detailed description of the company's most valuable customer, including characteristics, needs, buying behavior, and strategic importance. Used to focus marketing efforts and measure product-market fit.

---

## Orchestrator

A role that coordinates work across multiple Skills rather than performing specialist work. Orchestrators guide execution, validate quality, and ensure business alignment. Current Orchestrators include Team Leader, Reviewer, Legal Review, CMO, and Release Manager.

---

## Output Standard

Documentation that defines the expected format, quality, and completeness of deliverables from every Skill and Orchestrator. Output Standards ensure consistency regardless of the marketing task or AI platform used.

---

## Quality Gates

Validation checkpoints that work must pass before moving to the next stage. Quality Gates ensure quality is maintained continuously throughout the workflow, not only at the end.

---

## Self-Validation

The process by which each Skill verifies its own work before handing it to the next specialist. Self-validation includes verifying that objectives are complete, outputs are supported by evidence, recommendations are within scope, and confidence is assessed.

---

## Skill

A specialist AI role focused on a single marketing responsibility. Examples include Market Research, Product Positioning, SEO Strategy, and Content Marketing. Each Skill has a clearly defined scope, responsibility, and set of deliverables.

---

## Strategic Drift

Gradual deviation from defined strategy due to inconsistent execution, unclear ownership, or lack of governance. Agent-A prevents strategic drift through clear responsibilities, structured handoffs, and shared governance.

---

## Team Leader

The primary Orchestrator role responsible for governing the entire Agent-A system. Team Leader interprets requests, builds execution plans, routes work to appropriate Skills and Orchestrators, enforces governance, and owns final delivery.

---

## GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)

The process of optimizing content, structure, and approach for AI-powered search engines and generative AI platforms. GEO extends traditional SEO optimization to include AI-generated results and conversational search.

---

## EEAT

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. A framework used to evaluate content quality and expertise. EEAT is particularly important for marketing content that establishes credibility and trust with audiences.

---

## Evidence

Verifiable information used to support recommendations and conclusions. Evidence may include research, customer insights, competitive analysis, performance data, and business context. Agent-A prioritizes evidence-based recommendations over opinion.

---

## Positioning

The unique value proposition and market position of a company or product. Positioning defines how the company differs from competitors, what problems it solves, and why customers should care.

---

## Reviewer

An Orchestrator role that validates the quality and completeness of work. The Reviewer ensures deliverables meet quality expectations and align with the approved brief before work proceeds to Legal Review.

---

## Legal Review

An Orchestrator role that evaluates marketing work for compliance risks, legal exposure, regulatory considerations, and marketing claims. Legal Review reduces risk before publication.

---

## CMO (Chief Marketing Officer)

An Orchestrator role that provides strategic and business approval. The CMO evaluates whether work supports business objectives, maintains positioning consistency, and achieves market fit.

---

## Release Manager

An Orchestrator role that confirms publication readiness. The Release Manager verifies that all required reviews are complete, deliverables are publication-ready, documentation is complete, and release standards have been met.

---

## 10–80–10 Model

Agent-A's Human-Governed execution model: 10% Human Direction (define goals, create brief, approve plan), 80% AI Specialist Execution (execute with continuous validation), and 10% Human Governance (review, approve, publish).

---

## Modular Architecture

A system design where each component is independent and reusable. Skills do not depend on implementation details of other Skills; they communicate through structured outputs and handoffs. This makes the framework easier to maintain and extend.

---

## Transparency

The principle that work should be easy to review and understand. Specialists should explain why recommendations were made, what evidence supports them, what assumptions were made, and what limitations exist.

---

## Workflow

The structured sequence by which work moves through Agent-A from user request to final deliverable. The workflow follows the 10–80–10 model and ensures every output becomes context for the next stage.
